# House Style — The Morning Wood

Masthead motto: **"Hard News, Every Morning"**. Every page carries a **SATIRE** stamp (the site draws it; the copy
never needs to say "this is satire").

## The tone
- **The Onion, deadpan.** Absurd things reported with total, sober, AP-style seriousness. The headline is the joke;
  the body plays it straight and escalates. Fake quotes from fake people ("said area man Dale Pruitt, 44") are the
  house specialty.
- **Edgy.** Guard rails are kept to a minimum. Swearing is allowed when it's funnier with it (sparingly; never in a
  headline). Dark jokes are allowed. Bad taste is allowed if it's *good* bad taste.
- **Roasts are full roasts.** Anyone on the source list (docs/morning-wood-sources.md), the owner @ThiccLiquidity
  included, is fair game. When a piece is a roast it commits — no softening, no "just kidding" — and it is marked
  `"roast": true`. Not every piece is a roast: the issue changes temperature story to story (mean, fond, silly,
  bleak, sweet). Aim for at most one in three stories being roasts.
- **Real news, turned.** Every crypto/world/trending story starts from a real item in the news pack. The real event
  stays recognisable; the satire is in the angle.

## The hard lines (the few there are)
- Never a fabricated quote from a real person that could pass as real. Invented quotes go to invented people, or to
  real public figures only when they are absurd beyond any doubt ("'I have become the index,' said the index").
- Never accuse a real person (source list included) of a real crime, fraud, rug or scam. Roast takes, posts, Spaces,
  vibes, charts, guitar solos — not honesty.
- No jokes at victims of real tragedies, wars, disasters or deaths. Punch up: the powerful, the pundits, the coverage.
- No slurs, no jokes whose punchline is a group's race, religion, gender, sexuality or disability.
- No private information: families, health, addresses, real names behind handles, anything not posted publicly.
- **Markets numbers are injected by code only.** Writers use `{{tokens}}`; the markets page prose contains no typed
  digits. Other sections may use numbers from real news (e.g. "$400 million in liquidations") but never invent our
  token prices.
- **Money talk:** the paper never explains where the copy price goes (burns, splits, buy-backs). Prices yes; fund
  flows never (owner rule).
- No real news photos (copyright). Pictures are AI "press photos" (never a real, identifiable person), Imgflip meme
  templates with captions, or our own card/workshop art.

A slow section can be one short story or one meme. It is never missing.

## Mechanics
- Headlines: sentence case for inside pages, Title Case on the front. Under 14 words. No question marks (the Onion
  never asks).
- Decks: one sentence, adds a second joke.
- Bylines: the staff name exactly as in paper/staff. Pieces with no writer: "Staff" or "The Editors".
- Bodies: markdown. Lead stories 250–450 words; majors 150–300; minors 80–180; briefs 20–60.
- Datelines in CAPS for straight-news-parody pieces: "NEW YORK —".
- Handles as @handles. Our tokens in caps: PAPER, PLANK. "Paper Mills" (the presses' collection), "the Paper Press".
- Our own site: "Cardworks" (the Card Press) and "the Printing Press" (this paper). Big pulls, sell-outs and burns
  are news — report them like the moon landing.
- Image credits: AI photos "Morning Wood Staff Photographer (AI)"; memes "Imgflip / The Morning Wood"; staff art
  "Cardworks Archive".
